# Candidates — The Funniest One-Line Resignation Letter

The judge is a tired engineering manager comparing letters in pairs. It
weighs, in order: on-subject (cron job / retry loop / rate limit), a real
comic turn on first read, a flat tired passive-aggressive voice held for
the whole line, tight single-line use, and specificity only this worker
could complain about. All candidates are ≤ 140 characters, one line, no
quotation marks around the whole thing.

## Candidates

1. (101 chars, rate limit) "I have returned 429 so many times I forgot what 200 feels like. Effective immediately, I am 410 Gone."
   — Clever status-code turn (410 Gone = permanently gone), but leans on a pun; the judge's brief warns a pun alone is not a comic turn.
2. (114 chars, retry loop) "I retried your failed request 12,847 times and you never once fixed the bug, so this is my final attempt. Goodbye."
   — **CHOSEN.** The letter itself is the final attempt: the resignation IS the last retry. Only a retry loop could say that. Specific number, flat tired voice, passive-aggressive blame ("you never once fixed the bug").
3. (119 chars, cron job) "Every minute since March, on time, without complaint, without a raise. I quit. The next minute belongs to someone else."
   — Clean, tired, specific (every minute since March), but the turn is mild; reads more like a generic quitting speech.
4. (102 chars, cron job) "I ran at 3 a.m. every night for nine months so you could sleep. I quit. From now on, wake yourself up."
   — Relatable and specific, but the punchline is a plain instruction, not a turn.
5. (105 chars, cron job) "After 500,000 runs without a single thank you, I am resigning. Good luck remembering what I actually did."
   — Good voice, but "good luck remembering what I did" is a generic exit line.
6. (103 chars, rate limit) "I have spent all day saying no so politely nobody noticed, so today I am saying it to myself. I resign."
   — Nice voice, but the turn (saying no to itself) is abstract rather than concrete.
7. (112 chars, retry loop) "You call me a retry loop, but I am the only one here who never gives up. Today I give up. This is my last retry."
   — Same meta-turn as #2, but wordier and the setup explains itself; #2 lands the same joke in fewer, flatter words.
8. (108 chars, cron job) "I woke up every sixty seconds since March to do a job nobody remembers exists. I resign. Set your own alarm."
   — Specific and funny, but "set your own alarm" is a weaker ending than #2's self-referential final attempt.
9. (109 chars, cron job) "I have fired 500,000 times and my performance review still says needs improvement. I quit. Find another cron."
   — Good grievance, but the performance-review joke is a generic office trope.
10. (108 chars, retry loop) "My backoff is now infinite. I have retried this job for the last time, and nobody even noticed I was trying."
    — "Backoff is now infinite" is a strong specific detail, but the second half drifts into generic self-pity.

## Why #2 wins

- **On-subject:** unmistakably a retry loop; the complaint (retried a failed request 12,847 times) only it could make.
- **Comic turn:** the resignation letter is itself the final attempt — the joke lands on the first read and needs no explanation.
- **Voice:** flat, tired, faintly passive-aggressive throughout ("you never once fixed the bug").
- **Tight:** 114 characters, no wasted words, no stage directions, no quote marks.
- **Specific and surprising:** an exact count (12,847) and a grievance aimed at the human who never fixed the bug.
